UAE e-invoicing: what businesses need to know

The UAE is moving business invoices to an electronic system. The Ministry of Finance has set the first deadline for large businesses. Here is what has been announced so far.

Ask on WhatsApp View Business setup service

Who must start first

The Ministry of Finance says businesses in the e-invoicing system with annual revenue over AED 50 million must fully implement e-invoicing by 1 January 2027.

  • These businesses had to appoint an Accredited Service Provider (ASP). The ministry extended that deadline from 31 July 2026 to 30 October 2026
  • The ministry said the 1 January 2027 implementation date stays unchanged

Changes announced in October 2026

  • E-invoices, e-credit notes and their data must be kept for the period set by the Tax Procedures Law and given to the Federal Tax Authority on request
  • The earlier rule to store this data inside the UAE was removed
  • The rule to tell the FTA about a system failure within two business days was removed
  • Transactions between members of the same tax group are temporarily excluded for 24 months from 1 January 2027

Smaller businesses

The ministry's announcements we checked only set dates for businesses over AED 50 million. Dates for smaller businesses have not been covered here yet. We will update this page when we confirm them on official sources.

Questions

E-invoicing: common questions

When does e-invoicing start in the UAE?

The Ministry of Finance says businesses with annual revenue over AED 50 million must fully implement it by 1 January 2027.

What is an Accredited Service Provider (ASP)?

A provider accredited for the UAE e-invoicing system. Businesses over AED 50 million had to appoint one by 30 October 2026.

Do e-invoices have to be stored in the UAE?

No longer. In October 2026 the ministry removed the rule to store the data inside the UAE. It must still be kept and given to the FTA on request.

Are tax group transactions included in e-invoicing?

Transactions between members of the same tax group are temporarily excluded for 24 months from 1 January 2027.
