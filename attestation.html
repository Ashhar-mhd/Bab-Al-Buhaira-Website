Why attestation is needed

Documents issued outside the UAE usually need attestation before UAE authorities accept them, for example for a family visa, Golden Visa, job or school admission. This normally means attestation in the issuing country, then by the UAE embassy, then by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA).

We arrange attestation for documents from more than 100 countries, and legal translation into Arabic and other languages, including English, Malayalam, Hindi, Urdu and Tamil.

The attestation steps

  1. Check the original. Make sure the document is the original, is in good condition, and that the names match your passport.
  2. Attestation in the issuing country. The document is first attested by the authorities of the country that issued it. The exact offices differ by country and document. For Indian documents this ends with the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA).
  3. UAE embassy or consulate. The UAE embassy or consulate in that country then attests the document.
  4. UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA). Once in the UAE, the document is attested by MOFA. This is the step UAE authorities check.
  5. Legal translation. If the authority needs it in Arabic, a licensed legal translator translates it.

Some documents issued inside the UAE only need MOFA attestation. We check what your document needs before we start.

How long does it take?

It depends on the country and the document. MOFA attestation of a document already attested abroad is usually quick, while the full chain from the home country takes longer. We give you the timeline for your document.
