Corporate Tax registration in the UAE: the basics

Every company in the UAE must register for Corporate Tax, even if it pays no tax. Here are the basics for small businesses, and we can do the registration for you.

Ask on WhatsApp View Business setup service

Who must register for Corporate Tax?

  • Companies: the Federal Tax Authority (FTA) says all taxable persons must register and get a Corporate Tax Registration Number. This includes small companies with a trade licence.
  • Individuals doing business: a person must register if their business revenue is more than AED 1 million in a calendar year. Salary, personal investment income and real estate investment income are not counted.

Corporate Tax rates

  • 0% on taxable income up to AED 375,000.
  • 9% on taxable income above AED 375,000.

So a small business can be registered and still pay no tax. Registering is required either way.

Small Business Relief

A business with revenue of AED 3,000,000 or less, in the current and all previous tax periods, can choose Small Business Relief. It is then treated as having no taxable income for that tax period. Free zone companies that qualify for 0% cannot use it. We check if you qualify.

Penalty for late registration

The FTA charges an administrative penalty of AED 10,000 for late Corporate Tax registration. Under the FTA's waiver initiative, this penalty is waived if the first tax return is filed within 7 months from the end of the first tax period. Don't wait: register on time.

How registration works

  1. Log in to the FTA's EmaraTax portal with UAE Pass.
  2. Create a taxable person profile for the business.
  3. Fill in the Corporate Tax registration form and upload the documents.
  4. The FTA reviews it, usually within 20 business days.

The FTA does not charge a fee for registration. We can prepare and submit it for you.

What to send us

  • Trade licence
  • Emirates ID and passport of owners with more than 25%, and of the authorised signatory
  • Proof that the signatory is authorised (for example the memorandum or a power of attorney)
